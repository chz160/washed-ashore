## Synthesis (INFERRED from the above)
- Harvested, long-lived species (catfish, buffalo, drum, gar, bass, crappie): more large, old fish; numbers up moderately, capped by food and cover (flathead by cover units, MDC).
- Stock-dependent species fade: walleye, striped/hybrid bass and rainbow trout are gone or near-gone by 2012; sauger persists on natural reproduction at lower numbers; paddlefish rare but large.
- Nutrient inputs fall (no city sewage, farmland going to cedar and briar per the land brief): plankton-feeders (gizzard/threadfin shad) lose some biomass first, so the pool is a little less "busy" than today. This supports N3's "limited by food/habitat".
- No lockage: no up- or downstream passage; migratory skipjack and blue catfish depend on what was in the pool in 1987. Skipjack: uncommon. Blue catfish: present, low numbers, big.
- No channel maintenance: fallen trees accumulate along banks and at hollow mouths. More cover for flathead, bass and crappie (Archer 2019 notes the response to wood in channelised rivers is site-dependent, so this stays a modest, structure-only boost).
- Wariness drops (Goetze 2017; Januchowski-Hartley 2011): fish scatter at the low end of measured flight distances.
