## 1. Which species, and how 25 years change them (item 1, G1, G2)

### 1.1 The pool
Bells Bend sits in the Cheatham pool: a 7,450-acre riverine impoundment of the Cumberland from Nashville to Cheatham Dam (completed 1952), full pool 385 ft [1]. Fish come from TWRA's Cheatham page [1] (species and where anglers find them), TWRA's carp page [7], USGS NAS records [5][6] and, for life history, the Missouri Department of Conservation field guide [2] (Tennessee publishes no per-species pages; MDC covers the same Mississippi-basin populations).

### 1.2 Lore changes since 1987 (N3)
| Driver | Effect by ~2012 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No angling, commercial netting or bowfishing | More large, old fish in harvested long-lived species: catfish [10], bass [11], buffalo (can live past 100 years [13][14]), drum, gar. Marine no-take reserves show the same direction for biomass, density and size, with variation by taxon [9] | [9]-[14] |
| No stocking | Walleye, striped/hybrid bass and rainbow trout are stocked in Cheatham today [1]; trout can't survive summer [1]. All three are gone. Sauger is stocked too but reproduces naturally in the river: present, fewer | [1] |
| No lockage, no generation schedule | No up- or downstream passage [7][8]. Migratory skipjack herring and paddlefish depend on what was in the pool in 1987: skipjack uncommon (impoundments already reduced it [2]); paddlefish rare (they rely on hatcheries where dams block spawning [2]) | [2][7][8] |
| City sewage and farm runoff stop | Lower nutrients, so less plankton and fewer shad; planktivores respond first [15]. The pool is a little less busy than today: "limited by food/habitat" (N3) | [15] INFERRED |
| No snag clearing | Fallen trees build up along banks and at hollow mouths. More cover for flathead, bass and crappie; a modest, structure-only boost (wood response is site-dependent [16]) | [16] INFERRED |
| Wariness | Protection lowers wariness faster than it changes abundance [17]; flight distances sit at the low end [18][19] | [17]-[19] |

### 1.3 Carp ruling input (G2)
- **No silver or bighead carp were in the Cumberland in 1987.** USGS NAS's first Cumberland records are bighead 2002 (Lake Barkley) and silver 2011 (Cumberland City); the Cheatham pool records are bighead 2003 and silver 2017 (Sycamore Creek) [5][6].
- The escapes that seeded the Mississippi basin happened "in the late 1980s and early 1990s" during floods [7]; Ridgway et al. put establishment in the Ohio by the 1980s [3]. Either way the Cumberland was reached later.
- They reached Tennessee waters "via locks at Kentucky and Barkley dams" [7]; telemetry studies frame upstream passage as lock-chamber passage [4][8].
- With no lockage after 1987, Barkley (about 57 ft of head) and then Cheatham block the way to Bells Bend.
- **Recommendation: silver and bighead carp OUT. Common carp stands in** (in America since 1831, firmly established by the 1890s [2]). Common carp roll, tail and occasionally jump; their jumps stay rare and inside the surface-event cap. Plausible but off-map: silver carp thick below Barkley Dam by 2012, a hook for later content.

## 2. Distribution by band, mapped onto our bed (item 2)

### 2.1 The bands (rev F1: defined by bed depth only, per f-td ruling/fish-depth-bands)
Bed depth = WaterLevelY - terrain height via TerrainQuery.TryGroundHeight (WaterMaps G clamps at 4 m; LevelMaps shore distance is wrong near the north-line crossings). f-level confirmed every bank, bluffs included, has the same bed: 0.66 m at 3 m out, 1.94 m at 7 m, 3.05 m at 10 m, 7.3 m at 15 m, 10 m flat from 20 m. South of the north line there is no far bank (flat W-10 to the map edge). No fish north of the line.

### 2.3 Structure (rev F1: a density uplift and mix shift on the shared bed; director ruling/fish-robertson-island-anchor)
Structure area = the anchor's shoreline (f-level, sections/level-structure-anchors.md) x 0-20 m out, banded by bed depth like everywhere else. Inside it: adults = multiplier x band density x area; mix = band mix x preference weight, renormalised; flathead (structure-only) is added on top. No structure fish swims deeper than its bed allows. The game has no snags, riprap or piers, so there is no snag anchor (the 25-year snag build-up stays lore only).
