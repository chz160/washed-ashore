## Siting criteria

These are the Tennessee-specific criteria from the NRHP MPS (citing Ingle 2011, Good 1938 and CCC enrollee Hobart Parish):
1. **High ground within the forest being watched.** This was "usually" the highest elevation in the wooded area, on a high hill. It was not always the absolute summit; in the mountains, peaks under fog and cloud were avoided.
2. **Maximum unobstructed 360° coverage of fire-prone forest.** This was "of the utmost importance". Height was added (a taller tower) where trees or ridge shoulders blocked the view (Good 1938).
3. **Overlapping viewsheds with neighbouring towers.** Spotters at neighbouring towers were called on to fix a fire's location, using an Osborne Firefinder. CCC practice put towers 20-30 miles apart, and efficient visibility was 10-15 miles.
4. **Room for the tower, auxiliary buildings and an access road.** The road doubled as a firebreak. The three NGS sheets show the real pattern: towers sat 0.2-0.6 mi up a gravel spur off a highway or county road (GC2324, GC2560), or beside a gravel-road junction (GC2550).
5. **Little clearing needed.** Each leg sat on a concrete footing. A keeper's cabin usually stood at the base.
6. **Land tenure.** Sites were on public land or on private land under a 40-99 year lease.

I found no source that ties sites to being near a district office. The MPS says only that lookouts reported to the CCC camp or dispatcher.
