# IDECO "1933 model" / Aermotor MC-39 steel lookout tower: design digest (round 1)

Run date: 2026-10-10. All claims trace to sources retrieved this run. Main sources:

- **[MPS]** *Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975*, NRHP Multiple Property Documentation Form (NRIS 64501237). PDF: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/8986b1c9-543a-4d56-9482-166cde732e78/. - **[USFS-T1004]** USFS "Landing Details for Inside Stairs", Sheet 4 of 10. MPS Fig. 8, p. 40.
- **[USFS-T1006]** USFS "Stair Details", Sheet 6 of 10. MPS Fig. 7, p. 39.
- **[SEW]** Sewanee Fire Lookout Tower NRHP nomination (NRIS 15000116), Franklin Co., TN. PDF: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/2007215e-cb5d-4328-b850-d52b968f853e. Photos by Meghan Weaver, TVAR, 9 April 2014.

Key identity fact: the MPS (pp. 20, 25) says the 1933 International Derrick model "featured an interior dogleg steel-frame staircase that provided access to a trap door on the cab floor, and was identical to the Aermotor MC-39". It also says the model "is indistinguishable from the Aermotor line, save for the manufacturer's plaque." The MPS calls it "International Derrick Model 1933"; it does not use the name "IDECO MC-39". The USFS sheets match the IDECO drawing (the same height series and landing numbering), so you can model from either set.

## Stair and cab details

- **Configuration (the "diagonal step pattern").** Each flight rises diagonally across the tower interior, corner to corner. Seen in elevation (USFS "Diagonal views showing inside stairway", IDECO "View EE"), the flights form an alternating zig-zag dogleg. In plan, each landing is framed as a **square rotated 45°** inside the tower square, so the actual platforms are triangular corner pieces ("triangular landing ... of wood planks", MPS/SEW). Successive flights alternate direction, so the stair climbs around all four corners. A CCC builder recalled "wood steps going up about 8 to 10 steps and turning so that you balanced all corners of the tower" (MPS p.? quoting Justin 2003). Sewanee's first landing is at the NE corner (SEW).
- **Steps per flight, 99'9" tower, top to bottom:** 9 (cab), 9, 12, 14, 17, 18, 18, 18, 18. That is 133 steps in total (USFS-T1002). The upper flights are shorter because the panels shrink toward the top. For the 79'6" tower, the inference is that you drop the lowest flights. This is unverified.
- **Stair width:** 2'-0" between stringers (USFS-T1006).
- **Pitch:** 12 vertical in 9¼ horizontal (~52°), for all flights except the top flight, which is 8 in 12 (drawing note: "For all except top flight which is 8 in 12"). Tread spacing is shown as 9" (USFS-T1006). Unverified whether 9" is the rise or the going; check the zoomed drawing.
- **Treads:** 2" wood treads bolted onto 2x2x⅛ steel tread angles with ⅜x2½ carriage bolts and lock washers. The stringers are 2x2x⅛ angles (USFS-T1006; IDECO notes: "Lumber platforms, house floor and stair treads will be secured with ⅜ carriage bolts..."). Sewanee: "treads, consisting of wood" (SEW). So the "steel stair treads" in the brief means steel tread angles with wood treads; galvanized steel framing throughout.
- **Handrails:** 1½x1½x⅛ angle on 2x2x⅛ posts, on **both sides of all stairways** (USFS-T1002/T1006 note). Landing guard rail is about 3'-6" high (USFS-T1006 typical landing). Landings are framed with 2½x2½x3/16 angles.
- **Landing sizes:** 1st/2nd landing details show about 3'-1" and 2'-1⅜" dimensions; lower landings are marked 5'-2" (USFS-T1004). These are small triangular platforms. The MPS says these small landings and the bouncing doglegs drove the switch to the LS-40.
- **Cab entry:** the top flight lands on a "cab landing" angle frame and passes through a **trap door in the cab floor** (USFS-T1001 floor section; MPS).
- **Cab interior:** the drawings show only the trap door, the floor register and the double wood floor. The Sewanee team was not allowed into the cab. No source this run documents the Osborne firefinder pedestal, stool, or lightning rod for this model (see Could not find). The MPS says only generally that towers held "binoculars and an alidade, as well as a radio or telephone."

## Leads

- The other TN MC-39/IDECO nominations in the MPS are Big Hill, English Mountain (Cocke Co.), Kettlefoot (Johnson Co.) and Twinton. NPGallery records NRIS 15000832/15000833 and 100001828 turned up in search and may be some of these; their nominations may have taller (80–100 ft) examples and cab interiors.
- Fickes, C.R. 1935, *Forest Service Construction Handbook / Lookout structures*: the full 10-sheet T-1001…T-1010 set. Sheet 7 covers joints; others may cover the cab landing and firefinder stand.
- Starr 2011 and Starr et al. 2014 (cited by the MPS) on the Aermotor vs International Derrick history.
- The rest of the Sewanee photo log (photos 0001–0002, 0008, 0010) has wider context shots of the tower.
- The Forest Fire Lookout Association (firelookout.org) and its NHLR listings for TN towers. This run did not fetch them.
- University of Idaho "Keeping Watch" (Cold Mountain) describes the MC-39 as a 7x7 steel cab on an Aermotor tower, with the tallest at 179 ft. That conflicts with the MPS figure of 176 ft.

## Could not find

- The model designation "IDECO MC-39". Sources say "International Derrick Model 1933 = Aermotor MC-39". IDECO's own model number was not found, and the IDECO drawing title block shows only "E-4898".
- HAER/HABS measured drawings for any TN MC-39. None were searched successfully this run.
- Original IDECO or Aermotor catalogs.
- Cab interior photos: Osborne firefinder pedestal height, stool, map table, and lightning protection (rod or ground cables). None of this is documented in the sources retrieved.
- Exact landing plank layout, plank sizes, and confirmed tread depth/rise.
- Exact 1950s TN-specific variations. The MPS says TDF kept building MC-39 and LS-40 types "until ca. 1970" but gives no 1950s detail.
- Cab window sash dimensions per pane (derive from the 3'6" wall, the 4'0" band, the 7'0" width and a 3x3 grid per sash).
