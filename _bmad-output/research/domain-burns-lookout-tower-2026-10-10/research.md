## MC-39 design (for the Blender model)

- **Frame:** 4 angle-iron legs, 3×3×¼ at the top to 5×5×⅜ at the base. They taper straight from a **19'0½" square base** (79'6" tower) to **7'0" at the top**. X-bracing on every panel face, with a horizontal girt at each landing.
- **Stairs:** each flight runs diagonally from one interior corner to another, landing on small **triangular plank landings** in a frame turned 45°. 2'0" wide, 2" wood treads on steel angles, **pitch about 52°** (12 in 9¼), handrails on both sides. The last flight ends at a **trap door in the cab floor**. The 99'9" tower has 133 steps in 9 flights (scale down for 79'6").
- **Cab:** **7'0" square**: a 3'6" galvanized steel lower wall, a 4'0" window band, and **two 9-pane metal sashes per side** (one hinged, one fixed). Double 1" wood floor. **Pyramidal hip roof** at a 6:12 pitch, 8'0" square at the eaves, with a ventilator on top. A 12' flagpole on a corner post. Interior (not photographed anywhere): an Osborne firefinder on a pedestal and a stool, per period practice. That part is unverified.
- **Best references:** `burns-nhlr-lutes-main.jpg` and `-feb2023.jpg` (2023); YouTube VpTGZRPrPN0 (2016 GoPro climb; landings at 0:36–0:52, top at 0:58–1:10); `mc39-*` drawings (the 1935 USFS standard set is public domain).

## Placement on the Bells Bend map

There was **no historic tower in Bells Bend**. The nearest was Sullivan Ridge (36.111, -86.966; removed), about 8 km SW. Tennessee towers went on the **highest wooded hill**, with 360° views, room for a keeper's cabin, and an access road that also served as a firebreak.

| Candidate | Lat, lon | USGS elev | Nearest OSM road | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Central Ridge Summit** | 36.1655, -86.9065 | **244 m (highest in the bend)** | about 900 m (service track) | **Recommended:** central, a 360° view over both river arms |
| Ridge North Spur | 36.170, -86.908 | 234 m | about 905 m | Runner-up: broader shoulder |
| North Knob | 36.195, -86.922 | 240 m (noisy) | about 255 m | Too close to the north barrier (1.2 km) |

**The fiction:** after the collapse, the tower was moved here, or (simpler) "it was always here" in this alternate 1950s Tennessee. It's game fiction, not a historical claim.
