- (direction) Inputs: README.md and the writer's original pitch. Tensions to work: (1) slow skill gains vs permadeath skill wipe, (2) shared ecology vs griefing, (3) 24:1 time vs healing and offline state, (4) online-mandatory vs same-game-solo, (5) ambition vs scope
- (decision) Batch: TRIZ Contradiction, Persona Journey, Ecosystem Thinking, Worst Possible Idea, Kill the Crown Jewel
- (technique) started TRIZ Contradiction
- (decision) Goal confirmed: brutally hard, slow game where you can lose everything
- (decision) Rule: on death, intangibles (skills, XP) are wiped completely, with no partial carryover
- (idea) A new character can go back for the previous character's corpse and gear, unless someone else looted it first
- (idea) What survives death: gear on the corpse and any bases the character built
- (direction) Keep the session at concept level and skip item-level detail for now
- (question) Character creation is undecided: does the player choose (buff/nerf, 2 starting skills with smaller penalties) or get a random character (stats, sex, clothes, gear)? The writer mentioned random assignment verbally; unclear if he still leans that way
- (decision) The writer's written pitch outranks his verbal brainstorming. Character creation is chosen, not random: fixed starter kit, a buff/nerf pick, and 2 starting skills with smaller penalties
- (insight) Losing your skills is the real death penalty, worse than losing gear. Even if you get your gear back, the new character can't use it well
- (idea) Coming back to a server costs you your skills, not your access. No extra entry barrier like a queue or timer has been suggested
- (idea) Death penalty 1 of 4: lose all skills
- (idea) Death penalty 2 of 4: lose all items on you
- (idea) Death penalty 3 of 4: find your way back to where you died from memory. No minimap; the only maps are ones you find or draw yourself
- (idea) Death penalty 4 of 4: you may run into enemies while under-equipped and low-skilled
- (idea) Guns and ammo are so rare they work as currency for barter, not just as survival tools
- (idea) Almost any item can be traded, at NPC settlements or with other players
- (idea) Get your old gear back, haul it to a settlement and trade it for things that help the new character level faster
- (idea) Valuable items you bring in become ongoing credit at a settlement's market
- (idea) Bring something rare to a settlement and get provisional residency there
- (idea) Settlements could grant a deed or land rights in exchange for something valuable
- (insight) Recovered gear the new character can't use well is still valuable as trade goods. Death turns your old life into money to spend at settlements
- (idea) Settlements can be founded and run by players. What a settlement expects of its residents is whatever those players decide; the game sets no rules
- (question) Pitch has NPC settlements and the user describes player-founded ones. How do the two coexist?
- (decision) NPC settlements are part of the base game. They keep low-population servers from feeling empty
- (decision) Player-founded settlements are set aside as a possible later feature, out of scope for now
- (technique) started Persona Journey
- (idea) Quick character creation; first-time players want to get into the world fast
- (idea) Arrival cinematic: the player leaves a wrecked boat and lands on shore
- (idea) Optional tutorial for basic controls, about 10 minutes including the character menu
- (idea) After the tutorial, only the map and time constrain the player. No objectives are handed out
- (idea) Distant points of interest pull new players inland: a man-made silhouette against the natural landscape
- (idea) Inventory UI modeled on The Forest (Endnight Games, 2018)
- (idea) Spawn points are semi-random, and their scarcity varies; some shores are barren
- (idea) Almost everything is interactive: break branches, throw rocks, pull up tufts of grass
- (idea) Needs are signaled without a HUD: slower movement, stamina loss, stomach-growl audio and animation
- (idea) Smoke rising above a point of interest signals other survivors and draws players toward each other
- (idea) Players fortify old buildings with improvised perimeter fences made from rubble
- (idea) Corpses stay and decay in place (flies, half-decayed bodies); unexplained bodies create tension and story
- (idea) Proximity voice chat: players call out to each other and talk out loud in the world
- (idea) Starter food is tucked in bag side pockets. Bags have pockets that hold items, which rewards inspecting your inventory
- (idea) Veteran players teach new players in-world; the knowledge gap creates social moments
- (idea) A safe place to log off is valuable, and other players can offer it as a favor
- (idea) Players coordinate session times with strangers they meet, which builds lasting cooperative ties
- (insight) Logged-off characters stay in the world and can still die, e.g. of starvation. Where you log off matters
- (question) Offline time: at 24:1, a logged-off character goes about 23 in-game days without food, water or warmth per real day away. No solution yet
- (idea) Consider a 1:1 time ratio, as in Animal Crossing
- (idea) Logged-off characters stay in the world asleep, as in Rust, rather than vanishing
- (question) What happens when another player finds you asleep while you're logged off?
- (insight) Both 24:1 and 1:1 push the game toward narrow, different audiences, and neither sounds fun. 24:1 is a stress fest where players can't progress; 1:1 is dense, niche sim
- (decision) Fun comes first, even in a ruthless game. Separate from the conventional simulation genre
- (direction) User admires Elden Ring as a model of a punishing game that is still fun
- (idea) Stashes left while offline can be raided by other players or NPC raiders
- (idea) NPC raiders exist as a threat
- (idea) Cooperating players split the work: one scavenges, one crafts and cooks
- (idea) Spawn kit includes a camping tarp that works as a drag sled for hauling what won't fit in the bag
- (idea) Scavenging pattern: set a central drop point and ferry finds to it
- (idea) Heaps of decayed garbage you have to sift through by hand
- (idea) Doors show a minimal contextual icon: breachable, and which tool it needs
- (idea) Improvise tools from scrap, e.g. rusted flat bar stock as a pry bar
- (idea) Physical traversal animations: prying doors, squeezing through gaps, forcing locked cabinets
- (idea) Real, period-accurate firearms with authentic roll marks, e.g. Browning SA-22 in .22 LR
- (idea) Guns are found unloaded and ammo is found separately; loading is a deliberate step
- (idea) Low skill shows in gun handling: slow raise, heavy sway, sloppy aim
- (idea) Scarcity tempts betrayal; whether to share or take is an emergent moral choice
- (idea) Death sequence: blurred, bloody vision, falling to the ground, tunnel vision. The killer can speak to you as you die. Red 'EXPIRED' text
- (insight) Core emotional payoff: the triumph of finding hard-won loot. That feeling pulls players back even after a devastating death
- (insight) The quit moment for Dana is death after a big find. The memory of that triumph is what might bring her back

- (idea) A sleeping (logged-off) character can be looted by other players. The body stays asleep in place; it isn't removed
- (decision) Sleeping characters can be killed like anyone else, with no special protection or penalty

- (idea) A companion dog guards you while you sleep

- (technique) started Ecosystem Thinking
- (direction) Writer's Dana hour 3 scenario received (same server, new character after death)
- (insight) Player knowledge is what carries over between lives: Dana learns from Jed and from online reading. 'We can't take away your memory' works as designed
- (idea) Players research the game outside it (guides, wikis); community knowledge is part of the experience
- (idea) Nothing despawns. Materials break down over time at different rates by type
- (idea) Spawn points are random per life. A new character can wash up somewhere unrecognizable
- (insight) Random spawns plus no minimap can make getting back to your corpse nearly impossible. Decide whether that's intended or too harsh
- (idea) Biomes change how movement feels: a gravelly marsh with dense brambles hides the ground and slows you, unlike open woods
- (idea) Player-set traps persist and are indiscriminate. A spring-spear trap set for small game can impale a player
- (idea) Traps are faintly visible if you watch where you step
- (insight) Split attention is a core tension: watch your feet for traps or the horizon for ambush; you can't fully do both
- (idea) Injury feedback without a HUD: red pulsing at the screen edge by body region, vignetting for extreme stress, risk of passing out
- (idea) Being caught in a trap immobilizes you until you free yourself
- (idea) Contextual action icons over a wound offer choices, e.g. snap the shaft (two hands breaking a pencil) or extract it (pliers)
- (idea) Medical and physical actions use multi-tap or chord input sequences. Failing one causes pain and worsens the screen effects
- (idea) Real-world knowledge pays off: snapping an impaled object before removing it is the safer choice
- (idea) Leg injury forces limping and dragging the leg; standing up becomes a struggle
- (idea) Improvised tourniquet from a shirt plus a rod (e.g. a pipe pulled from a wall) as the windlass
- (idea) Environment pieces can be broken free for use (a pipe jutting from a wall) through an input sequence
- (idea) Tear clothing into bandages (socks)
- (idea) Bandages soak through; a re-apply prompt appears until the bleeding stops
- (idea) Action icons appear transparent when you lack the required tool and opaque when you have it
- (idea) Starter kit: basic outfit (shirt, pants, underwear, socks, shoes); 24L daypack with surplus MRE, 32oz steel canteen, 6x9 tarp, rubberized poncho, 90-degree flashlight with 2 batteries, Buck 110 folding knife; flip-top lighter
- (decision) Starter kit items begin at 50% condition and can be repaired and maintained
- (idea) Gear attachment points: a knife in the front pants pocket for quick access, a flashlight clipped to a pack strap or pants pocket
- (idea) Check gear works (e.g. the flashlight) before relying on it
- (idea) Pre-logout checklist under time pressure: fire, shelter, clothing, food plan
- (idea) Night work is harder even with a flashlight, so daylight is a resource to budget
- (idea) Fire crafting needs tinder, kindling and whole wood; paper scraps work as tinder
- (idea) Tarp tent: 3 lashings (cord or wire) plus 3 posts (sticks, branches, pipes), pitched next to the fire
- (idea) Break down bushes for kindling and branches. Icons show the harvest type (axe symbol)
- (idea) Cut wire into shorter lengths with the knife
- (idea) Abandoned traps keep working and catch game that anyone can take, while hinting that a trapper might return
- (idea) Low field-dressing skill wastes part of the yield
- (idea) Smoking meat preserves it and frees you to do other tasks while it cooks; a branch serves as a cooking utensil
- (idea) Keep half your food for the road
- (idea) Tend the fire before logging off
- (insight) Players taking real-life breaks mid-session leave the character exposed in the world, the same issue as logging off
- (insight) Success signal: Dana goes over her time limit willingly. Recovering from near-death to stability is the second big payoff, alongside finding loot
