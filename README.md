# Washed Ashore

A quarter century after the collapse, you wash ashore with nothing. Open-world, multiplayer, survival RPG with permadeath, realistic scarcity, and a world that remembers.

## Setting & Premise

- Open world, first-person, post-apocalyptic survival RPG
- Western Hemisphere, Earth, circa 2012
- Approximately a quarter century after the worldwide catastrophe(s) that ended civilization, society, and rule of law; the collapse began in 1987 and the world now exists in the aftermath
- The world still feels familiar and grounded: no supernatural, paranormal, or fantasy forces
- Threats come from four sources: other survivors, dangerous wildlife, the environment, and the player themselves
- The map begins as a realistic peninsula with urban, transitional, and wilderness regions

## Map Vision

- Modest but fully explorable world; every nook and cranny is reachable and meaningful
- No fake filler buildings, empty interiors, or ghost spaces
- The map should be small-to-medium in scale to allow dense detail and meaningful exploration
- Fast travel is unnecessary if the world supports traversal, vehicles, and movement-based progression
- The world is shaped around a peninsula setting, which supports both playability and mechanical clarity

## Settlements, Structures, and Spaces

- A handful of settlements of different sizes and purposes
- Abandoned ruins and broken infrastructure scattered across the world
- Raw wilderness that feels lived in, dangerous, and dynamic
- Structures should be large enough to feel human-scale in first-person while still preserving a sense of density and exploration
- The map can expand over time through updates rather than DLC, using major world obstacles and progression gates

## Story & Lore

The player begins adrift, washed ashore after a journey gone wrong or a life spent fleeing chaos. They do not arrive with a clean origin story. They are simply a survivor among many, carrying only what they could keep and the memory of whatever brought them here.

The collapse began in 1987, without a clear public warning. There was no single great war or obvious apocalypse. Instead, a mix of human action and natural disaster fractured civilization in ways that changed the world forever. By circa 2012, roughly a quarter century later, a few survivors endure in a hostile land shaped by ruin and consequence.

The world preserves fragments of the old order, but the truth of how it ended is buried in the wreckage. The player is not just surviving; they are searching for answers in a ruined world that may not want them to know.

## Gameplay Pillars

### 1. Starting the Game

- New players begin with a basic starter kit: clothing, footwear, essential tools, rations, and a means of carrying gear
- Character creation includes a buff/nerf choice, with a small advantage in a limited number of starting skills
- The survival loop begins with scarcity, risk, and the need to adapt

### 2. Skill System

- Skills are built through repetition and lived experience
- Progression reduces the penalties of inexperience rather than simply increasing raw power
- Examples include firearm handling, hunting, crafting, orienteering, and resourcefulness
- Traits are grouped into families such as Health, Might, Will, Finesse, Wit, Sense, and Peace
- Skills can scale through multiple families to allow specialization while preserving a grounded progression curve
- Early gains should be slow and meaningful, never feeling arcade-like or trivial

### 3. Injuries and Healing

- Injury matters. A shot anywhere other than a limb is often fatal without immediate intervention
- Falls and high-impact damage can be catastrophic
- Limb injuries create major mobility penalties and long-term consequences
- Healing is not instant; it requires care, time, rest, and nutrition
- Common issues such as infection, illness, cuts, sprains, poisoning, and hypothermia all matter
- The game should favor prevention and risk management over brute-force healing
- Splints, bandages, field treatment, and improvised medicine can be used, with practical application and time cost

### 4. Spawn Points

- Spawn logic should feel meaningful and grounded in the world
- A player may begin on a coast, near a settlement, or in a remote region depending on the design of the server and world state
- Spawn points should reinforce the importance of survival and distance from safety

### 5. Harvesting and Looting

- Looting should be immersive and tactile
- Opening containers, searching debris, checking fridges, picking through garbage, and handling harvested wildlife should all feel deliberate
- Harvesting is not just a button press; resources are processed, carried, and stored
- Animals, plants, and materials should be realistically disrupted and depleted
- Players should be able to carry, drag, and manage their inventory in a way that respects weight, space, and physics

### 6. Melee and Ranged Combat

- Most objects can be used as improvised weapons: branches, stones, tool handles, pipes, bones, hammers, and more
- Melee categories include natural weapons, fabricated weapons, and restored tools
- Projectile options include bows, slingshots, and blow darts
- Firearms are a rare and valuable necessity, not a casual commodity
- Realistic platform popularity and ammunition scarcity should shape the world

### 7. Inventory and Mobility

- Inventory should be realistic and restrictive
- Items should not fit into pockets if they are not physically plausible
- Bags, storage, and carrying methods matter deeply
- Over-encumbrance should result in serious mobility penalties and risk of falling or losing balance
- Large or heavy items may need to be dragged or hauled instead of carried

### 8. Environment and Permanence

- The environment should be interactive, destructible, and exploitable
- Trees can be felled, structures can be fortified or destroyed, and resources can be depleted
- Corpses and materials should leave traces in the world as part of the landscape
- The ecology and economy of the world should react to overharvesting and player activity
- Destruction should persist as long as the world state requires it

### 9. Animation and Feedback

- Animations should support immersion and practical survival interactions
- Actions such as bandaging wounds, cutting materials, foraging, opening containers, and moving heavy equipment should feel deliberate and visceral
- The visual language should support the tone of a brutal but believable world

### 10. HUD and User Experience

- HUD should be minimal by default
- The player should be encouraged to read the world and understand their situation through observation and direct interaction
- UI should stay secondary to immersion and survival focus

## Multiplayer Direction

- Multiplayer is mandatory and should remain part of the same core game loop as solo play
- A server model with limited population and a persistent world is the preferred direction
- Character death should be meaningful; upon death, a player reenters as a new character with consequences, a reset in world position, and a fresh survival start
- The world should not feel like two separate games; single-player and online play should share the same survival logic, systems, and world integrity

## Time Scaling

- The day/night cycle should scale at approximately 24:1 in-game-to-real-time ratio
- The world should eventually support seasons and shifting daylight patterns based on latitude and real-world solar cycles
- A future tool or system may calibrate the cycle to a chosen geographic location for realism and immersion

## Current Working Title

The project currently uses the title: Washed Ashore.

This name reflects the theme of the player emerging into a ruined, indifferent world with no certainty beyond survival. It is deliberately grounded and evocative, while still leaving room to evolve if a stronger identity is later chosen.

## Summary

Washed Ashore is a grounded, realistic, first-person survival RPG set in a post-collapse world where the player must endure, scavenge, adapt, and survive by any means necessary. The project emphasizes a compact but explorable map, meaningful progression, permanent consequences, and a world that remembers both the player and the damage they leave behind.
